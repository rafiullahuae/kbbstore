# Agency practices for small-budget DTC / beauty on Meta (2025-2026), as autopilot features

Research date: 2026-11-20. Lens: small-budget UAE K-beauty store on Meta.

Evidence labels used throughout:
- **[Platform]**: Meta's or a payment provider's own data. Self-interested, but first-party.
- **[Vendor]**: a tool or agency selling the practice. Directional only.
- **[Case]**: a single brand's result.
- **[Survey]**: consumer survey.
- **[Weak]**: LinkedIn posts or aggregators with no traceable source.

Method note: only search results were available. Direct page fetches failed for motionapp.com, commonthreadco.com and rockerbox.com, which are outside this session's allowed network hosts. Figures attributed to those sites therefore come from search-result excerpts, not full-page reads, and need verifying before they are quoted externally.

## Which practices have evidence of lifting sales or lowering CPA at small budgets?

### Takeaway
The best-supported levers are creative-side:
- concept diversity inside a consolidated account;
- creator / partnership ads;
- iterating on proven winners.

Payment-side, BNPL availability is the strongest GCC lever. Almost all of the evidence comes from platforms or vendors; I found no independent, small-budget-specific controlled study. Offer mechanics (bundles, gift-with-purchase, free-shipping thresholds) are standard agency practice, but every quantified lift I found is a vendor claim.

### Cited Findings
**Account structure and creative diversity (Andromeda era)**
- Agencies consistently advise fewer campaigns and fewer ad sets, with broad targeting and many distinct creative concepts. Meta dropped its old "no more than six ads per ad set" guidance. — [Jon Loomer, Meta Andromeda](https://www.jonloomer.com/meta-andromeda/) [Practitioner]; [Jon Loomer, creative diversification](https://www.jonloomer.com/meta-andromeda-creative-diversification/)
- Claimed Meta internal test: one ad set with 25 diverse creatives produced 17% more conversions at 16% lower cost than five ad sets of five creatives each. I could not find Meta's original publication. — reported via search results summarising Andromeda guides (e.g. [Chatterbuzz](https://www.chatterbuzzmedia.com/blog/meta-andromeda-creative-targeting/), [Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/creative-targeting-ai-driven-delivery.md)) [Vendor, second-hand]
- Near-duplicate ads (the same product shot with copy tweaks) are treated by delivery as effectively one ad. Agencies now count *concepts*, not ads, where a concept differs in visual, message and audience. — [Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/meta-andromeda-creative-diversification/); [Top Growth Marketing, 7 changes for DTC](https://topgrowthmarketing.com/meta-andromeda-update/) [Practitioner]
- The volume caveat for small budgets: an ad set needs about 50 optimisation events a week to stabilise, so creative volume must match budget. — [AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/data-driven-thinking/what-metas-andromeda-update-actually-changes-and-what-it-doesnt/) [Trade press]

**Creator / partnership ads**
- Meta's figure: partnership ads average about 19% lower CPA and 13% higher CTR than standard brand ads. — via [Superfiliate](https://www.superfiliate.com/field-guide/video/the-role-of-creator-programs-in-your-marketing-strategy-by-maurice-rahmey); [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/meta-expands-partnership-ads-turn-creator-content-performance) [Platform]
- Meta EMEA paper: across 20 conversion-lift studies, putting at least 30% partnership ads into business-as-usual campaigns cut median cost per *incremental* purchase by 45%. A critic notes the evidence is mostly Meta's own analyses and the threshold wording varies between pages. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/meta-says-partnership-ads-cut-incremental-purchase-costs-45-in-emea-tests/) [Platform, critiqued]
- Agentio analysed about $130M of spend across 65,000 partnership ads. It found only about 5% lower CPA than the same UGC licensed and run from the brand handle. By placement:
  - Search: 63% lower CPA, 143% higher CVR.
  - Stories: 25% *higher* CPA.
  - Winning creator ads fatigued after about 36 days, after which CPA rose about 1.8x.
  — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/meta-partnership-ads-outperform-licensed-ugc-across-130m-usd-in-ad-spend-report-finds/); [Agentio](https://www.agentio.com/blog/infinite-creative-engine) [Vendor, large sample]
- Billo: creator ads had 11% lower CPA than other video ads in the same accounts. — [Billo](https://billo.app/?p=54984) [Vendor]
- Jones Road Beauty (Motion AMA), single-brand numbers:
  - Control campaign: 25.75% thumbstop, 5.83% hold.
  - Influencer campaign: 39.88% thumbstop, 17.03% hold.
  — Motion material surfaced via [Motion key creative metrics](https://motionapp.com/blog/key-creative-performance-metrics) [Case]

**Winner concentration (why systematic testing matters)**
- Motion Creative Benchmarks 2026 covered more than 550k ads from more than 6,000 advertisers, about $1.3B of Facebook and Instagram spend from September 2025 to January 2026. Only about 5% of ads reached 10x the account's median spend, so spend concentrates on a few winners. — [Motion Creative Benchmarks 2026](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/) [Vendor, large dataset; full report not read]

**Click-to-WhatsApp (CTWA) ads**
- Arabian Automobiles (Nissan, Dubai) ran an A/B test from 17 to 30 April 2025. Combining click-to-WhatsApp ads with click-to-website ads beat either destination alone, giving more leads at lower cost per lead. Not e-commerce. — [WhatsApp Business success story](https://business.whatsapp.com/resources/success-stories/arabian-automobiles-company) [Platform case]
- Non-UAE e-commerce CTWA cases using purchase optimisation:
  - 1.8x more sales and 40% lower cost per purchase (Malaysian group).
  - 28% more sales and 21% lower cost per purchase (VIP Leather Craft).
  — [WhatsApp Business stories](https://whatsappbusiness.com/?p=5183) [Platform case]
- Alyasra Fashion (Kuwait-based retailer) used CTWA ads to solve WhatsApp opt-in and claims 30x tracked ROI. — [Infobip](https://cdn-web.infobip.com/uploads/markdown-exports/www.infobip.com/customer/alyasra-fashion.md) [Vendor case]

**Offers**
- Bundle ads raised AOV 23-38% when added alongside single-product campaigns. No data shown. — [MHI Growth Engine](https://mhigrowthengine.com/blog/dtc-bundle-ads-strategy/) [Vendor]
- Skincare gift-with-purchase example: buy two cleansers and get a free travel-size lotion and retinol, framed as an AOV and retention driver. Not tied to Meta results. — [BOGOS / Swissvita](https://bogos.io/swissvita-malaysia/) [Vendor case]
- Median facial-skincare account AOV is about $47.47. — [Madgicx Labs, facial skincare](https://madgicx.com/labs/industries/beauty-personal-care/facial-skincare.md) [Vendor dataset]
- Free-shipping thresholds:
  - Common rule: set 15-30% above current AOV.
  - Claimed result: 15-25% AOV lift.
  - Health and beauty average minimum order for free shipping: $61 (ParcelLab 2025, cited second-hand).
  - Critique: the threshold can conflate revenue lift with profit lift.
  — [Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/ecommerce-growth/free-shipping-thresholds); [Eightx](https://eightx.co/blog/free-shipping-aov-threshold); [Branvas](https://branvas.com/blogs/news/free-shipping-threshold-by-category) [Vendor]

### Inferences
- For a small account, the practical recipe is:
  - one or two campaigns: a sales campaign plus a testing lane;
  - a few broad ad sets;
  - a steady flow of *distinct* concepts, rather than many variants of one.
  An autopilot should score how similar new creative is to existing ads and refuse near-duplicates.
- Partnership ads are worth building in, but expect roughly 5-19% CPA gains, not 45%. Avoid Stories placements for them, and plan to refresh them at about 30-36 days.
- With only about 5% of ads becoming spend winners, the system's job is to find and iterate winners fast, and to kill losers cheaply.

### Gaps
- I found no controlled study isolating these practices at under about $3k a month in spend.
- I found no K-beauty or UAE-specific Meta case study with disclosed spend.
- The Meta "25 creatives vs 5x5" test could not be traced to a Meta primary source.

## Creative analytics: how agencies tag and analyse creatives, and decide iterations

### Takeaway
Agencies use a standard metric stack:
- hook (thumbstop) rate = 3-second plays / impressions;
- hold rate = 15-second plays or ThruPlays / 3-second plays;
- CTR, CVR and CPA.

They read this stack against tags in the ad name: concept, angle, persona, format, hook type, creator and iteration version. Most agencies decide on a 70/30 split of iterations to new concepts, and judge against *their own account median* rather than industry benchmarks.

### Cited Findings
- Hook rate is 3-second video plays / impressions on Meta (2-second views on TikTok). It is not a default Ads Manager column, so it has to be built as a custom metric. — [Madgicx glossary](https://madgicx.com/glossary/hook-rate); [Motion glossary](https://motionapp.com/library/glossary/thumbstop-rate) [Vendor]
- Hold rate definitions differ, so check which one a benchmark uses before comparing:
  - Motion: 15-second plays / 3-second plays.
  - Billo: ThruPlays / 3-second plays.
  - Kickbite: share of 3-second viewers reaching 100% of the video.
  — [Billo](https://billo.app/blog/hook-rate-to-hold-rate); [Kickbite](https://www.kickbite.io/en/blog/hook-rate) [Vendor]
- Motion's tiers:
  - Thumbstop: below 25% weak, 25-35% solid, above 35% strong.
  - Strong e-commerce Meta creative: 30-40% hook, 25%+ hold, 1.5%+ CTR.
  — [Motion](https://motionapp.com/blog/key-creative-performance-metrics) [Vendor]
- Other hook-rate benchmarks conflict:
  - 2026 Meta average of 25.44% across 88,329 sales video ads.
  - Median 28-30%, with top performers above 40%.
  - Range of 15-35%.
  — [AdManage](https://admanage.ai/blog/what-is-a-good-hook-rate-for-facebook-ads); [Kickbite](https://www.kickbite.io/en/blog/hook-rate) [Vendor]
- Motion's own webinar says beating your account average matters more than industry benchmarks. — [Motion talk](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/creative-analytics-explained-why-your-ad-won-or-lost-evan-lee-motion/) [Vendor]
- Naming conventions:
  - OMR's template: date_audience_placement_product_approach_concept_element, plus a tag for "new" vs "iteration" of an existing concept.
  - BigEye example: `03-30-2026_UGC_ProblemHook_Casual_RedCTA_v2`.
  — [OMR](https://omr.com/en/reviews/contenthub/creative-testing); [BigEye](https://www.bigeyeagency.com/insights/build-creative-testing-framework-that-scales) [Practitioner]
- Testing structure: an ABO testing ad set per concept, with fixed equal budgets so delivery cannot starve a variant before it has data. Winners graduate to the scaling campaign. — [OMR](https://omr.com/en/reviews/contenthub/creative-testing); [Sovran](https://sovran.ai/blog/creative-testing-framework-meta-ads) [Practitioner]
- Iteration mix rule of thumb: 70% iterations of winners, 30% new concepts. This is one practitioner's heuristic. — [Growth Gems #126](https://growthgems.substack.com/p/growth-gems-126-creatives-and-paid) [Practitioner]
- Diagnosis logic:
  - Low hook rate means a new first three seconds (new hook) on the same body.
  - High hook but low hold means the body or story is the problem.
  - High hold but low CTR or CVR means the offer, CTA or landing page.
  Motion's help centre has a view built specifically for "which creatives need a new hook". — [Motion help](https://help.motionapp.com/en/articles/8757799-see-which-creatives-need-a-new-hook); [Imagine](https://www.imagine.art/blogs/hook-rate-vs-hold-rate) [Vendor]

### Inferences
- Autopilot feature: enforce a machine-parseable ad-name schema at creation time. A suggested schema: `date_concept_angle_persona_format_hook_creator_offer_vN`, including language AR/EN for GCC.
- Aggregate CPA, hook and hold by each tag, so the owner sees, for example, "routine-angle beats ingredient-angle" rather than which single ad won.
- Iteration rules keyed to the diagnosis above, with each ad compared to the account median, not an industry benchmark.

### Gaps
- I found no beauty-vertical or GCC cut of hook and hold benchmarks. Motion's full 2026 report was not readable from this session.
- I could not verify Foxwell Digital's specific framework from search.

## Post-purchase surveys and first-party attribution without big budgets

### Takeaway
A "how did you hear about us?" (HDYHAU) survey on the thank-you page is the cheapest first-party attribution layer. Agencies read it alongside MER and contribution margin rather than platform ROAS. Formal incrementality at small budgets is limited to:
- Meta's self-serve Conversion Lift, where the spend floor is disputed;
- detecting only large lifts;
- or crude on/off and geo tests.

### Cited Findings
- HDYHAU survey on thank-you and order-status pages (Fairing). Fairing claims at least 60% of its average client's customers respond. This is not independently verified. — [Fairing](https://fairing.co/blog/the-post-purchase-survey-a-vital-marketing-attribution-tool); [Fairing FAQ](https://fairing.co/resources/measurement-faqs/what-is-a-post-purchase-survey) [Vendor]
- Triple Whale merges survey answers with pixel data. It prefers pixel attribution, falls back to the survey answer, and can double-credit Meta and TikTok. — [Triple Whale KB](https://kb.triplewhale.com/en/articles/6377642-integrating-with-fairing-formerly-enquirelabs) [Vendor]
- CTC works top-down from contribution margin and does not optimise Meta ROAS first:
  - Healthy contribution margin: 20-35% of net sales.
  - Strong contribution margin: 35-50% of net sales.
  - MER is treated as a business-level metric.
  - CTC claims its client brands grew 35% top-line and 41.5% in contribution margin in 2025 (self-reported).
  — [CTC contribution margin guide](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/the-complete-guide-to-ecommerce-contribution-margin); [CTC Prophit Engine](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/why-we-built-the-prophit-engine) [Practitioner/Vendor]
- Blended MER floor is tied to gross margin: at 45% margin you usually need MER of 3.5x or more. Benchmarks are given for $5-50M brands. — [Eightx](https://eightx.co/blog/average-mer-by-ecommerce-vertical) [Vendor]
- Incrementality at small budgets:
  - Meta Conversion Lift: self-serve, minimum seven days, default 10% holdout. — [Haus](https://www.haus.io/article/facebook-incrementality-testing)
  - Spend floor cited as $5k by [Madgicx](https://madgicx.com/blog/meta-incrementality-testing) vs $10-50k+ by [Single Grain](https://www.singlegrain.com/blog-posts/analytics/incrementality-testing-playbook-math-types-runbook/). The sources conflict; check Meta's Experiments tool.
  - Google cut its incrementality-test minimum from about $100k to about $5k in 2025. — [Top Growth Marketing](https://topgrowthmarketing.com/?p=49209)
  [Vendor]
- Statistical power (95% confidence, 80% power): the control group needs about 1,650 purchases to detect a 10% lift, about 430 for 20%, 200 for 30% and 80 for 50%. Small accounts can only detect big effects. At 50% margin, a 4x reported ROAS pays only if at least half the sales are incremental. — [Top Growth Marketing](https://topgrowthmarketing.com/?p=49209) [Vendor]
- Geo-holdout design guidance:
  - CTC suggests 6-8 weeks for geo tests.
  - Eightx says holdouts are rarely worthwhile under about $5M revenue.
  — [CTC](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/how-to-measure-incrementality-in-ecommerce-the-framework-behind-3b-in-revenue); [Eightx](https://eightx.co/blog/what-is-incrementality-testing) [Practitioner]
- Beauty case: Hourglass Cosmetics validated Meta with a six-week geo holdout, pausing Meta in 10 holdout DMAs. Results were not readable from this session. — [Rockerbox](https://www.rockerbox.com/customers/how-hourglass-cosmetics-validated-metas-true-impact-with-a-geo-holdout-test) [Vendor case]

### Inferences
- Autopilot feature set:
  1. A native HDYHAU question on the order-confirmation page, in AR and EN, with "Instagram/Facebook ad", "Influencer (name)", "TikTok", "Friend/WhatsApp", "Google" and "Snapchat" as options. Snapchat is relevant for GCC.
  2. A daily MER and contribution-margin view using revenue, COGS, shipping, payment fees (BNPL fees are high), COD failure cost and ad spend.
  3. A "Meta-reported vs survey-reported vs MER" triangle in the weekly report.
  4. Scheduled pulse tests: pause or halve spend for one to two weeks and watch MER. Label these as low-power; they are not true lift tests.
- A UAE-only geo holdout across emirates is probably too small to read reliably. Prefer Meta Conversion Lift if the account qualifies.

### Gaps
- I found no published HDYHAU response rates for GCC or Arabic-language stores.
- I found no small-budget (under $5k a month) incrementality case study in beauty.
- Hourglass result figures were not retrievable.

## GCC-specific conversion levers (BNPL, COD, Arabic, delivery thresholds, trust, Ramadan/Eid, WhatsApp)

### Takeaway
BNPL (Tabby/Tamara) is the best-documented GCC conversion lever. Provider-reported conversion uplift is about 11-33% and AOV uplift about 21%, all from vendor data.

The other levers:
- COD is declining but still a trust safety net.
- Ramadan shifts shopping late at night and lifts order volume sharply.
- Free shipping is a top stated frustration.
- WhatsApp is a viable ad destination.

Evidence for Arabic-language landing pages is old or small-sample.

### Cited Findings
**BNPL**
- Trendyol (Turkish retailer, Tabby case): conversion rate +11%, AOV +21%, and about 40% of purchases via Tabby. — [Tabby case study](https://tabby.ai/case-studies/trendyol) [Platform case]
- Other BNPL conversion claims:
  - Tabby: about 33% average conversion jump.
  - Industry: up to 35% conversion and 50% basket uplift.
  - Tamara: about 15% online conversion uplift for partners.
  All are reported in coverage of an Amazon Payment Services whitepaper. — [tbreak](https://tbreak.com/bnpl-mena-amazon-payment-services-report/) [Vendor]
- Checkout.com's Tabby page claims +18% conversion. BNPL adoption among online shoppers is 39% in the UAE and 42% in KSA. — [Checkout.com](https://www.checkout.com/fr-fr/payment-methods/tabby); [Checkout.com newsroom](https://www.checkout.com/newsroom/checkout-com-and-tabby-partner-to-expand-bnpl-solutions-for-retailers-in-the-uae-and-saudi-arabia) [Vendor]
- Tamara names beauty as a high-demand BNPL category with larger baskets. Its claim is qualitative, with no beauty numbers. — [Tamara beauty](https://tamara.co/en-sa/industry/beauty) [Platform]

**COD and trust**
- UAE COD usage is down 53% since 2020 (Checkout.com/YouGov fifth MENA report, 18,000 adults). Another outlet says 60% regionally. Regional COD preference fell from 41% (2020) to 20% (2023). — [Checkout.com newsroom](https://www.checkout.com/newsroom/checkout-coms-4th-annual-mena-report-finds-cash-on-delivery-usage-halved-amongst-maturing-digital-economy); [Consultancy-me](https://www.consultancy-me.com/news/10931/online-shopping-now-a-mainstream-channel-for-a-third-of-uae-and-ksa-consumers); [Gulf Business](https://gulfbusiness.com/en/2024/retail/e-commerce-drives-mena-digital-payments) [Survey]
- 74% of UAE consumers would pay by card if COD were not offered. The report year is unclear. — [Arabian Business](https://www.arabianbusiness.com/industries/retail/uae-sees-140-percent-surge-in-monthly-online-shopping-report) [Survey]

**Free shipping**
- Tabby surveyed 7,500+ UAE and KSA shoppers. 82% had at least one online-shopping frustration, and missing free shipping frustrated 23% in the UAE and 29% in KSA. — [Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/2024/01/tabby-survey-82-shoppers-online-shopping-frustrating) [Survey, 2024]

**Ramadan / Eid**
- Tamara: order volumes up to +60% in KSA and the UAE in the first week of Ramadan 2025. 61% of KSA and UAE consumers shop directly via Instagram and TikTok during Ramadan, and one in four bought on influencer trust. — [Tamara Ramadan 2026 playbook](https://tamara.co/en-ae/blog-post/the-ramadan-2026-playbook-turn-business-insights-into-revenue) [Platform]
- Ramadan 2025 (Flowwow/Admitad): UAE orders +20% and GMV +13%; KSA sales +30% and GMV +35%. In Ramadan 2026, 18:00-00:00 was the peak MENA window, while KSA peaked 00:00-06:00 (31%). — via [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/uae-ramadan-2026-retail-shifts-to-multiple-shopping-windows-as-average-spend-climbs-10) and Ramadan coverage [Vendor data via press]
- Visa: in KSA and Kuwait, the share of transactions between 22:00 and 04:00 more than doubles in Ramadan. — reported in [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/ramadan/how-ramadan-reshapes-spending-habits-across-the-uae) [Platform via press]
- Meta/YouGov: 71% of UAE shoppers (60% in KSA) say personalised product and gift suggestions make Ramadan shopping easier. — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2254816) [Survey]
- Wider context: ArabyAds/Ipsos 2026 Ramadan Consumption Shifts report; YouGov Ramadan 2025 insights. — [Ipsos PDF](https://www.ipsos.com/sites/default/files/ct/news/documents/2026-02/2026-Ramadan-Consumption-Shifts-Report-by-ArabyAds-and-Ipsos_1.pdf); [YouGov](https://yougov.com/reports/51437-ramadan-2025-consumer-insights) [Survey; not read in full]

**Arabic language**
- 88% of shoppers prefer to buy in their native language. The source is a 2018 Rbbi study, cited second-hand. — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/sectors/retail/websites-must-strive-to-speak-the-language-of-this-region-1.2252129) [Weak/old]
- About 300 Emiratis preferred Arabic advertising despite high English competence. — [CORE paper](https://core.ac.uk/download/480762344.pdf) [Academic, small]
- Purchase intention rose with degree of website localisation (87 participants). — [WARC](https://www.warc.com/newsandopinion/news/how_global_should_localise_for_ecommerce/42601) [Academic, small]

**WhatsApp**
- Meta reportedly moved WhatsApp Business pricing from per-conversation to per-message on 1 July 2025. Verify this against Meta's pricing documentation. Vendor claims of 3-8x ROAS for UAE CTWA ads are unverified. — [Message Central](https://www.messagecentral.com/blog/whatsapp-marketing-uae) [Vendor]

**Before/after policy (beauty creative)**
- Meta's official pages restrict before-and-after imagery for weight loss and for cosmetic *procedures*, and bar ads that create negative self-perception. Third-party guides say everyday cosmetics and skincare are outside this scope, but that is unconfirmed on Meta's pages. — [Meta personal health & appearance](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/personal-health-and-appearance); [Meta health & wellness](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/health-wellness); [AuditSocials](https://www.auditsocials.com/blog/meta-beauty-cosmetics-ads-2026-before-after-photos-appearance-claims-policy) [Platform + Vendor, conflicting]

### Inferences
- Autopilot GCC defaults:
  1. Bake BNPL instalment messaging into ad copy and the landing page ("4 interest-free payments of AED X with Tabby/Tamara"). Make it a testable angle tag.
  2. Show the free-delivery threshold and its progress in ads and in the cart. Set the threshold at about 15-30% above current AOV and A/B test it.
  3. Arabic-language variants of top concepts with RTL landing pages, tagged `lang=AR`, to measure directly, since published evidence is weak.
  4. A Ramadan mode: shift budget and scheduling towards after Iftar until about 2am, pre-load gift sets and Eid bundles, and use personalised gift-finder creative.
  5. CTWA as a second destination for high-consideration routines or skin consultations, run alongside website ads.
  6. Show the COD and trust badges, and track COD return or refusal rate in contribution margin.
- Beauty-specific, policy-safe proof alternatives to before/after: texture shots, a dermatologist or creator explaining ingredients, review screenshots, routine demos ("AM/PM routine"), and "day 1 vs day 28" self-filmed diaries by creators. Each needs checking against Meta's current rules, plus UAE advertiser-permit rules where they apply.
- Routine bundles (cleanser, toner, serum, SPF), mini or sample add-ons as gift-with-purchase, and replenishment reminders (WhatsApp or email at about the product's usage cycle) are the standard beauty offer playbook. I found practitioner descriptions but no quantified case for them.

### Gaps
- I found no independent (non-provider) A/B test of BNPL on-site messaging in the UAE.
- I found no quantified data on K-beauty in the GCC, Arabic vs English ad performance on Meta, or Eid-specific conversion.
- I found no published influencer-whitelisting (partnership ads) results specific to the GCC.
- Replenishment and sampling evidence: no quantified 2025-2026 sources found in this pass.
- Unreachable hosts: motionapp.com, commonthreadco.com and rockerbox.com. Their content here comes from search excerpts only.
