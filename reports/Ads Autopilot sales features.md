# Guard the money path, then learn concepts

**Plain-language summary.** KBB Ads Autopilot already covers most of what the paid tools sell: rules that stop losers and scale winners, creative generation, fatigue checks, a season calendar, research, and judging ads on real shop orders and refunds. The features that would add the most sales from here are not more ad-making. The first is a **money-path guard**: stop or warn when an advertised product sells out, when its price in the feed no longer matches the site, when Tabby, Tamara or Stripe start refusing payments, or when Meta's tracking stops matching real orders. Every dirham spent while one of those is broken is wasted, and Meta's own tests credit good server-side tracking with **13% lower cost per result** ([Meta Blueprint](https://agencies.facebookblueprint.com/student/path/514683/activity/466137)). The second is **learning by concept, not by ad**: tag every ad by angle, hook, format, language and offer, measure how many viewers stop scrolling and keep watching, and refuse near-copies before launch, because Meta's delivery now treats near-duplicates as one ad ([Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/meta-andromeda-creative-diversification/)). The third is **knowing which buyers are new**, so the app stops paying Meta to "win" customers who would have reordered anyway. Practitioners report that uncapped Advantage+ sales campaigns spend 40–70% on existing customers or warm engagers ([TrackBee](https://www.trackbee.io/blog/how-to-stop-wasting-meta-ad-budget-on-returning-customers)). After those come a profit-and-repeat view, catalog clean-up (especially an Arabic catalog), reviews and creator ads, a one-question "how did you hear about us?" survey, and offer tests that stay inside the owner's rules: GLOW and the free gift only. Several of these need a website package because the shop's autopilot API currently exposes only orders, daily totals, refunds/cancellations and products ([verified in repo] `routes/autopilot-api.php`). Almost none of the evidence is independent. Meta's figures are Meta's, tool figures come from vendors, and no controlled study at a small UAE budget was found, so each item below carries an evidence label. Meta's developer pages, motionapp.com and commonthreadco.com were blocked in this research session; figures from them come from search-result extracts and need checking before anyone quotes them.

> **Evidence labels.** **[Meta]** = Meta's own docs or figures (first-party, self-interested). **[Independent]** = academic or non-vendor research (Google/Deloitte, Baymard, Spiegel). **[Vendor]** = a tool or agency selling the practice. **[Practitioner]** = agency or expert opinion. **[Extract]** = known only from a search-result extract of a page this session could not open. **[Repo]** = read in this repository on 20 Nov 2026. **[Inference]** = the research team's reasoning, not a sourced fact.

## Stop paying for clicks the shop cannot convert

**1. Money-path guard.** The app already avoids picking out-of-stock products when it builds new ads ([Repo] `engine/brain/brain.go`, `pickProducts`), but a search of the rules engine found no check that pauses or swaps an ad that is **already running** when its product sells out. Meta stops showing out-of-stock catalog items only after it ingests the feed or API update ([Meta catalog fields](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/commerce-platform/catalog/fields/)) [Meta, Extract], and practitioners report feeds that still show sold-out items as in stock after a failed sync ([AdAdvisor](https://adadvisor.ai/blog/automate-out-of-stock-exclusion-meta-catalog)) [Practitioner]. Single-product ads and collection ads built from fixed product lists do not benefit from catalog automation at all. The guard should compare live ads' products with the shop's live stock and price every few hours and propose a pause or a swap. It should also flag a `sale_price` without an end date, because Meta keeps it live until removed ([Meta catalog fields](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/commerce-platform/catalog/fields/)) [Meta, Extract].

The second half of the guard watches the checkout. UAE shoppers lean heavily on BNPL: adoption among online shoppers is 39% in the UAE ([Checkout.com](https://www.checkout.com/newsroom/checkout-com-and-tabby-partner-to-expand-bnpl-solutions-for-retailers-in-the-uae-and-saudi-arabia)) [Vendor], and digital wallets held 43.92% of UAE e-commerce payments in 2025 ([Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/united-arab-emirates-ecommerce-market)) [Vendor]. A refused Tabby or Tamara start turns a paid click into nothing. The shop already records the HTTP status and provider code of every refused payment start (CLAUDE.md, "Payments are frozen") [Repo], so the website package only needs a **read-only** endpoint that counts refusals per gateway per hour. It must not touch `app/Services/Payments/`, which is frozen. The app would alert, and propose holding budget increases, when a gateway's refusal rate jumps. A third signal, orders per visit by landing page and device against a 28-day baseline, needs visit counts the shop's autopilot API does not expose today. Baymard's research shows why the checkout deserves watching: average documented cart abandonment is 70.22%, and design fixes alone are worth about 35% more checkout conversions on the average site ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/research/checkout-usability)) [Independent]. The guard does not redesign the checkout. It stops the app from blaming the creative when the real fault is the money path. Effort is small in the app and small-to-medium for the website endpoints.

**2. Signal-health watch.** This has the strongest evidence of anything in the report, and it is cheap because the shop already sends CAPI events with refunds and retries (2.60.678). Meta's Blueprint material reports a **13% cost-per-result improvement** for CAPI plus pixel across 28 experiments in 2022 ([Meta Blueprint](https://agencies.facebookblueprint.com/student/path/514683/activity/466137)) [Meta]. A later figure of **17.8% lower cost per result** across 5,926 advertisers is attributed to Meta, but only secondary sources were found, and its baseline is "no CAPI at all" ([Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/meta-pixel-conversions-api-ai-updates-2026); [WeltPixel caveat](https://weltpixel.com/blogs/news/metas-one-click-conversions-api-what-it-does-and-what-it-leaves-out)) [Unverified]. The shop has the integration, so the gain still available is keeping it healthy. Meta's Dataset Quality API exposes event match quality and diagnostics programmatically ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/conversions-api/dataset-quality-api)) [Meta, Extract]. Only matched events optimise delivery, and deduplication works only within 48 hours ([Meta CAPI best practices](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/conversions-api/best-practices)) [Meta, Extract]. The app should compare Meta's daily purchase count with the shop's real orders, track match quality and dedup over time, and alert on a gap. It should also check the catalog match rate, meaning how many purchase `content_ids` exist in the catalog. Meta's profit optimisation requires that rate above 90% ([Meta: value optimisation for profit](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/ads-commerce/conversions-api/guides/value-optimization-for-profit/overview)) [Meta, Extract]. A silent tracking break makes every other rule in the app optimise on wrong numbers, which is why this ranks second despite producing no visible feature.

## Learn which ideas sell, not which ad IDs won

**3. Concept tags and hook/hold analytics.** Every serious creative tool recurs on this: Triple Whale's Creative Cockpit breaks spend and ROAS down to concept and hook ([Triple Whale](https://TripleWhale.com/creative-cockpit)), Madgicx labels creative elements and tracks fatigue ([Madgicx](https://madgicx.com/products/creative-insights)), and Motion and Foreplay Lens sell the same view ([Creatify on Motion](https://creatify.ai/de/blog/motion-pricing-(2026)-plans-credits-and-what-you-ll-actually-pay)) [Vendor]. The app already has fatigue rules (7-day CTR drop, cost rise, frequency 2.5 or more; [Repo] `engine/brain/rules.go`). It has no hook-rate or hold-rate measure; a search of the engine for "hook rate" and "thumbstop" found nothing. Hook rate is 3-second plays divided by impressions, and it is not a default Ads Manager column ([Madgicx glossary](https://madgicx.com/glossary/hook-rate)) [Vendor]. Hold-rate definitions differ between tools (15-second plays or ThruPlays over 3-second plays), so the app should pick one and stick to it ([Billo](https://billo.app/blog/hook-rate-to-hold-rate)) [Vendor].

The value is in the diagnosis agencies use. A low hook rate calls for a new first three seconds on the same body. A high hook with low hold means the story is weak. Strong hold with poor clicks or sales points at the offer, the call to action or the landing page ([Motion help](https://help.motionapp.com/en/articles/8757799-see-which-creatives-need-a-new-hook)) [Vendor, Extract]. Each ad is compared with the **account's own median**, not an industry benchmark ([Motion talk](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/creative-analytics-explained-why-your-ad-won-or-lost-evan-lee-motion/)) [Vendor, Extract]. The benchmarks themselves conflict anyway, from a 25.44% average to a 28–30% median ([AdManage](https://admanage.ai/blog/what-is-a-good-hook-rate-for-facebook-ads)) [Vendor]. The app should write a machine-readable tag set into each ad name at creation time (date, concept, angle, persona, format, hook type, creator, offer, language AR/EN, version), following agency naming schemes ([OMR](https://omr.com/en/reviews/contenthub/creative-testing)) [Practitioner]. It should roll results up per tag, so the plan can say "routine angle beats ingredient angle in Arabic" and feed that into the self-learning loop (#25). Motion's 2026 benchmark of more than 550,000 ads found only about **5% of ads reach 10x the account's median spend** ([Motion Creative Benchmarks 2026](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/)) [Vendor, Extract]. A small account finds its few winners only if each test teaches something about a concept. A common heuristic splits new work 70% iterations of winners and 30% new concepts ([Growth Gems](https://growthgems.substack.com/p/growth-gems-126-creatives-and-paid)) [Practitioner, one source]. Effort is medium: insights pulls for video play fields, a tag parser, and a rollup screen.

**4. Concept-diversity gate.** Under Meta's Andromeda retrieval, agencies count concepts rather than ads, because near-duplicates (the same product shot with tweaked copy) are delivered as effectively one ad ([Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/meta-andromeda-creative-diversification/); [Top Growth Marketing](https://topgrowthmarketing.com/meta-andromeda-update/)) [Practitioner]. A claimed Meta test found one ad set with 25 diverse creatives produced 17% more conversions at 16% lower cost than five ad sets of five, but no Meta original was found ([Chatterbuzz](https://www.chatterbuzzmedia.com/blog/meta-andromeda-creative-targeting/)) [Vendor, second-hand]. A reported "Creative Diversity" rating in Ads Manager from August 2026 is unverified against Meta ([Common Thread](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/metas-new-creative-diversity-score-is-live-in-ads-manager-what-ecommerce-brands-must-act-on-now)) [Extract]. The app's consolidation playbook (#24) already implies this. The missing piece is a pre-launch check that scores a new creative against the live pool on visual, message and persona, then refuses or flags a near-copy and suggests a genuinely different angle. The volume caveat matters at small budgets: an ad set needs about 50 optimisation events a week to stabilise, so the pool should stay small and distinct, not large ([AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/data-driven-thinking/what-metas-andromeda-update-actually-changes-and-what-it-doesnt/)) [Trade press]. Claude already sees the pool when it creates, so effort is small.

## Buy new customers, and count profit the way the shop banks it

**5. New vs returning buyers.** Uncapped Advantage+ sales campaigns reportedly spend **40–70% on existing customers or warm engagers** ([Dataslayer](https://www.dataslayer.ai/blog/meta-value-rules-2025-what-they-are-how-they-work-and-why-they-increase-costs); [TrackBee](https://www.trackbee.io/blog/how-to-stop-wasting-meta-ad-budget-on-returning-customers)) [Practitioner]. For a K-beauty shop with repeat buyers and an email programme, that is spend on sales the shop would largely have made anyway. Meta's Value Rules let an advertiser raise or lower bids for chosen customer types ([Social Media Today](https://socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-ads-value-rules-rundown-overview/756764)) [Meta via trade press]. Beauty brand Laura Geller optimised on a first-time-purchaser event and reported **+46% ROAS** against its acquisition campaigns ([CustomerLabs](https://www.customerlabs.com/blog/meta-ads-value-set-optimization/)) [Vendor case, single brand]. The shop knows from its order history whether each buyer is new, but the autopilot API does not pass that on. The website package should add a first-order flag to `/orders` and a hashed, consent-gated customer-list export. The app would then report **new-customer orders and new-customer cost** next to MER, keep an existing-customer audience in sync for Meta's existing-customer definition and caps, and use an exclusion audience of recent buyers on prospecting ([Common Thread](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/meta-exclusion-only-audiences-ecommerce-2026)) [Practitioner, Extract]. It deepens #22, where the app already builds website and engagement audiences and lookalikes ([Repo] `engine/model/model.go`). The earlier research flagged UAE PDPL consent as the gate on sending hashed customer data, so the list sync must stay behind a consent switch. Effort is medium. Impact is high, because it changes what the budget buys.

**6. Profit truth.** The app already computes contribution per order from margin, shipping, payment fee and refunds ([Repo] `engine/brain/economics.go`). What it lacks is the business-level view agencies steer by. Common Thread works top-down from contribution margin, treating 20–35% of net sales as healthy, and uses MER as the business metric rather than Meta ROAS ([CTC](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/the-complete-guide-to-ecommerce-contribution-margin)) [Practitioner, Extract]. Triple Whale sells the same in its Founders Dashboard, including LTV by first-order cohort over 365 days ([Triple Whale KB](https://kb.triplewhale.com/en/articles/5725663-cohorts)) [Vendor]. Three additions matter for this shop. First, put the real cost of the **free gift with every order** and the per-method payment fee into contribution; BNPL fees vary widely and the shop's own contract rates should be used. Second, track COD orders that close uncollected; the shop's reconciliation already counts them ([Repo] `CashOnDeliveryPosition.php`). Third, add a 60–90-day **repeat rate by first product and by acquisition month**, so the app can raise the allowable cost per new customer for products that bring buyers back. That last needs a hashed customer key on the orders API. Agencies pair this with an MER floor tied to gross margin, with about 3.5x MER needed at 45% margin ([Eightx](https://eightx.co/blog/average-mer-by-ecommerce-vertical)) [Vendor, benchmarks from $5–50M brands]. Effort is medium. The impact is that the daily budget level, the one decision the app owns outright, is set on money the shop keeps.

## Make the traffic convert: catalog, proof, offers, pages, flows

**7. Catalog quality pack (website package).** Meta's catalog checklist asks for clean descriptions, correct price format, `sale_price` on every sale item, `additional_image_link`, and `sale_price_effective_date` ([Meta catalog best practices](https://developers.secure.facebook.com/documentation/ads-commerce/commerce-platform/catalog/best-practices)) [Meta, Extract]. The biggest gap for a mostly Arab-women audience is that Arabic ads currently draw on one feed. Meta supports **language and country override feeds** keyed to the same ids, carrying only the changed fields ([Meta localized catalog](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/catalog/localized-catalog/localized-catalog-setup/)) [Meta, Extract], so Arabic catalog and collection ads can show Arabic titles and land on `/ar` pages. Add an `estimated_margin` field now as preparation, but do not depend on profit optimisation yet: vendors quote about 200 purchases in 28 days as the entry bar, and it is still in beta ([Triple Whale KB](https://kb.triplewhale.com/en/articles/16305474-optimize-meta-campaigns-for-profit-with-profit-optimization); [Stape](https://stape.io/blog/meta-profit-based-campaign-optimization)) [Vendor]. One owner rule applies here. Meta can draw a strikethrough price and a %-off label on catalog ads from `sale_price` ([Top Growth Marketing](https://topgrowthmarketing.com/?p=48754)) [Practitioner], which conflicts with "no percentage discounts in ads", so those overlays must stay off in the executor. The earlier research also found that advertised discounts need authority consent in the UAE.

**8. Reviews into ads.** Spiegel Research Center found products with five reviews had **270% higher purchase likelihood** than products with none, with the first five mattering most ([Spiegel](https://spiegel.medill.northwestern.edu/How-Online-Reviews-Influence-Sales)) [Independent, 2017, correlational]. The shop has reviews. The app should add review count and rating to the products feed it reads (website package), give products with five or more reviews a boost in `pickProducts`, and generate review-quote creatives from real reviews only. It should also ask the website to prioritise review requests for advertised products still under five reviews. No Meta documentation was found for star-rating overlays on catalog ads, so reviews belong in the creative, not in a feed field. Real customer words are also a policy-safe alternative to before/after imagery, which Meta restricts in several forms ([Meta personal health and appearance](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/personal-health-and-appearance)) [Meta, Extract]. Effort is small.

**9. Creator / partnership ads.** The executor already supports the partnership format ([Repo] `engine/brain/formats.go`). What it lacks is the workflow that makes it pay. Meta says partnership ads average about **19% lower CPA and 13% higher CTR** ([eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/meta-expands-partnership-ads-turn-creator-content-performance)) [Meta], and a Meta EMEA paper claims a 45% lower cost per incremental purchase when at least 30% of ads are partnership ads, with critics noting it rests on Meta's own analyses ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/meta-says-partnership-ads-cut-incremental-purchase-costs-45-in-emea-tests/)) [Meta, critiqued]. An independent-of-Meta dataset of about $130M across 65,000 partnership ads found only **about 5% lower CPA** than the same UGC run from the brand handle, **25% higher CPA in Stories**, and fatigue after about **36 days** ([Net Influencer on Agentio](https://www.netinfluencer.com/meta-partnership-ads-outperform-licensed-ugc-across-130m-usd-in-ad-spend-report-finds/)) [Vendor, large sample]. The realistic gain is 5–19%. The app should keep a creator register (permission, partnership code, expiry), schedule refreshes at 30–36 days, steer partnership ads away from Stories, and tag creator as a concept dimension in feature 3. The cost is the creators themselves, which is why effort is medium.

**10. "How did you hear about us?" survey (website package).** One question on the order-confirmation page, in Arabic and English, is the cheapest first-party attribution layer. Options should cover Instagram/Facebook ad, an influencer (with name), TikTok, Snapchat, Google, and friend or family. Fairing claims at least 60% response, which is unverified ([Fairing](https://fairing.co/blog/the-post-purchase-survey-a-vital-marketing-attribution-tool)) [Vendor]. The app would show a weekly triangle of Meta-reported, survey-reported and MER. Triple Whale's integration shows the trap: merging survey answers with pixel data can double-credit channels ([Triple Whale KB](https://kb.triplewhale.com/en/articles/6377642-integrating-with-fairing-formerly-enquirelabs)) [Vendor], so the survey should be a check, not an input to bids. The order-received page is a checkout-adjacent view, so the package must leave payment code untouched. Effort is small.

**11. Offer tests inside the owner's rules.** Evidence for offer mechanics is the weakest in this report: free-gift and threshold lifts of 12–25% AOV, and gift-with-purchase claims of +67% to +115%, are vendor anecdotes without controls ([Intelligems](https://www.intelligems.io/resources/blog/should-i-offer-gift-with-purchase); [GrowthSuite](https://www.growthsuite.net/blog/free-shipping-thresholds-increase-aov)) [Vendor, weak]. That argues for testing, not assuming. Within the owner's rules (GLOW and "free gift with every order", no percentages) the testable levers are a **gift tier** (the standard gift on every order, a better gift above about 15–30% over current AOV), **routine bundles** (cleanser, toner, serum, sunscreen) as a creative angle, and a **BNPL instalment line** ("4 interest-free payments of AED X with Tabby/Tamara"). Tabby and Tamara report conversion uplifts of 11–33% and AOV gains around 21% ([Tabby Trendyol case](https://tabby.ai/case-studies/trendyol); [tbreak](https://tbreak.com/bnpl-mena-amazon-payment-services-report/)) [Vendor]. Missing free shipping frustrated 23% of UAE shoppers in a Tabby survey ([Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/2024/01/tabby-survey-82-shoppers-online-shopping-frustrating)) [Survey]. The app's part is tagging `offer=` in feature 3 and reading **profit per visitor**, not AOV alone. The website's free-gift system already exists, so a tier is a settings change if it supports thresholds. Whether a gift tier changes the owner's promise of "a free gift with every order" is his call (see open questions).

**12. Ad-to-page match.** No controlled study of message match or dedicated landing pages for Meta traffic was found, so this ranks below the measured levers. Speed evidence is solid: a 0.1-second mobile speed gain lifted retail conversions 8.4% and AOV 9.2%, but only when every page in the journey improved ([web.dev / Deloitte](https://web.dev/case-studies/milliseconds-make-millions)) [Independent, 2019]. The shop's speed is frozen by the owner, and the right feature is a **read-only speed watch** on the pages taking the most ad spend, not a speed project. For the creative side, the app should send each concept to its best-matching destination (the product for a single-product ad, a routine or brand collection for a routine angle) and send Arabic ads to `/ar` pages. Purchase intent rose with website localisation in a small academic study ([WARC](https://www.warc.com/newsandopinion/news/how_global_should_localise_for_ecommerce/42601)) [Independent, 87 participants]. The UAE ranked first globally for mobile commerce, with 67% using a phone for their last purchase ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=2699875)) [Survey], so every check here runs at phone width.

**13. Retention coordination.** Abandoned-cart flows have the highest revenue per recipient of any flow, $6.77 per email on average in Klaviyo's 2026 data ([Klaviyo](https://www.klaviyo.com/blog/abandoned-cart-benchmarks)) [Vendor, cross-merchant]. The shop already runs abandoned-cart email, so paid retargeting of the same people in the first day or two may double-spend. The app should suppress recent buyers from retargeting immediately and run a **holdout**: split abandoners at random into "email flow only" and "flow plus retargeting", then compare orders ([SegmentStream](https://segmentstream.com/blog/articles/debunking-post-view-attribution)) [Practitioner]. The 24–72-hour window is an inference, not a sourced number. A **replenishment reminder** timed to each product's usage cycle is standard beauty practice, but no quantified 2025–26 evidence was found for it. It belongs in the shop's email marketing (website package), with the app reading which first products drive repeat orders (feature 6).

## Cheap extras with honest limits

**14. Opportunity-score proposals.** Meta's Marketing API now exposes the 0–100 opportunity score and its recommendations ([Meta dev blog, Nov 2025](https://developers.facebook.com/blog/post/2025/11/10/unlock-peak-performance-with-new-opportunity-score-features-in-the-marketing-api/)) [Meta, Extract]. Meta reports a **12% median drop in cost per result** for adopters in testing ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-expands-opportunity-score-ai-ad-reccomendations/747716/)) [Meta, self-reported], while saying a high score "does not guarantee" performance. The app should read the recommendations daily and turn them into proposals that pass through its own rules and learning-phase discipline, never apply them blindly. Many recommendations push for more ad sets or more spend, which the consolidation playbook resists. Effort is small.

**15. Incremental view and pulse tests.** Meta claims its Incremental Attribution setting produced a 46% average lift across 37 lift studies, and that rules-based attribution undervalues Meta by a median 31% ([ppc.land](https://ppc.land/metas-suite-of-truth-framework-rewrites-how-advertisers-measure-ad-impact/)) [Meta]. Haus, which sells measurement, found Meta's 7-day click metric under-reports incrementality by 15% ([Haus](https://haus.io/blog/meta-is-changing-its-attribution-settings-heres-what-you-need-to-know)) [Vendor]. Platform numbers can be wrong in either direction. Statistics limit what a small shop can learn: detecting a 20% lift needs about 430 purchases in the control group ([Top Growth Marketing](https://topgrowthmarketing.com/?p=49209)) [Vendor]. The feature is therefore modest. Show Meta's incremental column, if the account has it, next to click-only and shop MER, and schedule labelled, low-power budget steps (halve or pause for one to two weeks, read MER). It deepens the MER work in #27.

**16. Competitor watch list.** The app's research already asks Claude about UAE K-beauty competitors "via Meta Ad Library pages if reachable" ([Repo] `engine/brain/research.go`). Automation cannot go further through official means: outside the EU and UK, the Ad Library API returns only political and social-issue ads ([Meta Ad Library API](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/api)) [Meta, Extract], and commercial UAE ads show no spend or reach anywhere ([ShortsIntel](https://www.shortsintel.com/guides/meta-ad-library-api)) [Vendor]. The useful addition is a curated list of competitor pages, a direct Ad Library link for each, and a log of what the owner or Claude sees there. An ad that has run a long time is the only available proxy for a winner. Scraper APIs exist ([Apify](https://apify.com/thenetaji/meta-ad-library-api-scraper)) [Vendor] but sit outside Meta's official API with terms-of-service risk, which is a poor trade for an account the earlier research showed is hard to recover once restricted.

## What not to build

Several things the paid tools sell are either done by Meta already or cost too much at this budget. **Audience selection, placement optimisation, budget shifting inside a campaign, and basic copy, image and translation variants** are what Advantage+ automates ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/meta-ai-driven-advertising-system-andromeda-gem-468020)) [Trade press]. The app's job is Arabic cultural QA of Meta's AI outputs, not re-implementing them. Haus's 640 tests also found Advantage+ beat manual campaigns in only 42% of head-to-heads, from a sample averaging about $1M a month ([Haus](https://www.haus.io/blog/the-meta-report-lessons-from-640-haus-incrementality-experiments)) [Vendor], so the app should keep watching Meta's automation, not trust it.

**Multi-touch attribution and media-mix suites** (Northbeam from about $1,500 a month, Triple Whale's paid tiers around $549 and up; [Costbench](https://www.costbench.com/software/marketing-attribution/northbeam/); [PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/triplewhale)) [Vendor aggregators] would mostly re-derive what the app already gets from real shop orders. **Smartly** is positioned for six-figure monthly spend, with a reported minimum of about $4–5k a month ([Superscale](https://superscale.ai/alternatives/smartly/pricing)). **Geo-holdout tests** need many comparable regions and 6–8 weeks; a UAE-only shop cannot power one ([Eightx](https://eightx.co/blog/what-is-incrementality-testing)) [Practitioner]. **Paid creative generators** such as AdCreative.ai showed no independent sales-lift evidence, and creation already runs on the owner's Claude Max.

The owner's decisions rule out three items the agency research recommends. **Click-to-WhatsApp ads** have platform case studies (+28% sales at one leather retailer; [WhatsApp Business](https://whatsappbusiness.com/?p=5183)) [Meta case], but he does not offer them. **Percentage or strikethrough discounts**, including Meta's catalog sale overlays, stay off. **Dayparting** is out as well: the earlier research found no controlled evidence it helps on Meta, and it forces lifetime budgets. Ramadan's late-night peak, with Tamara reporting order volumes up to 60% higher in the first week ([Tamara](https://tamara.co/en-ae/blog-post/the-ramadan-2026-playbook-turn-business-insights-into-revenue)) [Meta-adjacent platform], is better served by the existing season calendar's budget lift ([Repo] `engine/brain/season.go`). Finally, **Meta profit optimisation via `estimated_margin`** should be prepared in the feed but not built upon until the shop clears roughly 200 purchases in 28 days.

## Open questions for the owner

Seven answers would firm up the ranking. **How many orders does the shop take in a typical 28 days, and how many come from ads?** That decides whether profit optimisation, Conversion Lift or even weekly tag-level conclusions are possible, or whether tags must be read over a month. **Is there consent to send hashed customer emails and phones to Meta for customer lists?** Feature 5 depends on it, and the earlier research flagged UAE PDPL. **Is "a better gift above AED X" acceptable alongside "a free gift with every order",** and is BNPL instalment wording ("4 payments of AED X") acceptable in ads, given it is a payment message rather than a discount? **Are creators available, and at what cost,** for partnership ads? **Should the money-path guard pause ads by itself** when a gateway fails or a product sells out, or only alert him? The earlier design put pause-only actions in the automatic tier. **What are the real Tabby, Tamara and Stripe fee rates and the cost of the free gift?** Profit truth needs them. **Does the shop record visits per landing page anywhere**, or would site conversion monitoring need new counting that must respect the speed freeze?

Three verification items remain from blocked sources. Meta's Dataset Quality API fields, opportunity-score API behaviour and localized-catalog codes were read from search extracts only. Motion's hook/hold views and benchmark figures, and Common Thread's exclusion-audience and contribution-margin guidance, were also extracts. And the "Creative Diversity" rating, the 17.8% CAPI figure and the "25 creatives vs 5×5" test have no Meta primary source.

## Conclusion

The comparison with commercial tools turns out to be reassuring. The app already matches or beats them on automation and creation, and it knows the shop's margins, which none of them does. What separates a good small-budget account from a wasteful one is not a cleverer optimiser. It is refusing to spend while the shop cannot sell (out of stock, a gateway down, tracking broken), and making every test teach a reusable lesson about which ideas sell to Arab women in Arabic and English. The second-order insight is that the highest-value website work is not a new storefront feature but a set of small, read-only data endpoints (first-order flag, payment-start refusals, review counts, visits by page) that let the app see the money path. That keeps the frozen payment, speed and header code untouched while giving the autopilot the facts it needs to buy new customers at a profit.
