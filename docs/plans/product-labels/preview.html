How the real picker stays this smooth — and why this way.
  • A small search index, sent once. When the label editor opens it loads one compact list of every product: id, name, brand, SKU, published Arabic name, categories, price and a 64px thumbnail path. About 3,000 products ≈ 100 KB gzipped, built once on the server and cached until a product, brand, category or Arabic name changes; the browser keeps it too (ETag), so the second visit downloads nothing.
  • Then every keystroke is answered in the browser — no request per keystroke, not even a debounced one, so typing never waits on the network. The timer next to the count is the real time each search takes (about 1 ms at 300 and under ~10 ms at 3,000 on this machine — tick "Feel the full catalogue").
  • Why not the existing search endpoints? The admin's current product searches (Product tabs, New Arrivals, Catalog) each do LIKE '%word%' over name/SKU and stop at 20–30 rows; they cannot match words in any order, do not see the Arabic name, and a round trip per pause would feel slower than this on Cloudways. The index also reuses the shop's own Arabic normalisation (أ/إ/آ → ا, ة → ه) and synonym groups (spf → sunscreen, cica → centella) so "wide range upon searching a word" is the same range the shop search gives.
  • The list draws 50 rows at a time ("Show 50 more"), so even "pick all 1,200 found" never builds a huge page. Categories and counts come from the same index, so the green total is computed instantly, with no request.
  • Saving sends rules, not product lists: the picked ids, the ticked categories, "include sub-categories" and the excluded ids. One request, on Save only.
  • If the catalogue ever grows past ~20,000 products the same box switches to one debounced request (250 ms) against an indexed search table, with no change to how it looks.

How labels look in the shop

Four looks. In each, your labels stack in the top-start corner (top-left in English, top-right on /ar), up to two per card; the automatic sale pill keeps its own corner, so "Best Seller" and "-20%" never fight for the same spot.

Look:
Side by side — the same product with an automatic sale badge and your "Best Seller" label
Shop grid with look A — 4 across on a computer, 2 on a phone, as the shop draws them
Product page with look A — on the main photo and beside the title
Next to the automatic badges (proposal E below). A custom label takes the top-start corner. The automatic sale pill (-20%) stays top-end where it is today; the automatic New / Bestseller pills give way when a product has a custom label, so a card never shows "Bestseller" and "Best Seller" twice. Sold out always wins the top-end corner. Nothing changes on a product that carries no custom label — the shop is byte-identical until the first label is saved.

What to choose (reply with the letters, e.g. "A, E, I, K")

  1. APill — the shop's own badge shape. BRibbon — flush to the photo edge. CCorner sash — diagonal across the corner. DSoft tag — pastel, quieter. (Or "per label": each label picks its own shape in the editor — that control is already drawn above.)
  2. ECustom labels first, automatic sale stays in the other corner, automatic New/Bestseller give way (recommended). FCustom labels replace every automatic badge on that product. GAutomatic badges first, custom labels after.
  3. HOne label per card (highest priority wins). IUp to two (recommended). JUp to three.
  4. KCatalog → Labels, and the automatic badges stay at Growth & Marketing → Product Labels with a link between them (recommended). LOne screen, Catalog → Labels, with two tabs: Your labels and Automatic badges; the old sidebar entry goes.
