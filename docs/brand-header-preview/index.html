Brand page header: six options

A, B and C follow your brief. The logo sits on the banner with the brand name beside it and the description underneath, all inside a panel about 60% of the width so nothing merges with the picture. On a phone the logo and name stay on the banner and the description moves under it.

D, E and F are my own ideas. They are deliberately different from your brief. I recommend D.

Each option is shown at laptop width (scroll it sideways if your screen is narrower) and at phone width (390px).

Logo shape: on the shop this is a setting in each brand's header popup. Try both here.

Your briefMy ideaRecommended
A

Frosted glass panel

Your brief

A soft white panel with a light blur, so the banner still shows through faintly. Dark text, easy to read on any banner colour.

B

Deep brand-tint panel

Your brief

The panel takes a dark shade of the brand's own colour, with white text. It looks richer and blends with the banner without merging into it. On a phone the description sits on a pale tint of the same colour.

C

Centred white card

Your brief

A solid white card centred on the banner, with logo and name on one row and the description underneath. It is the calmest of the three. It hides more of the picture's centre, so it suits banners whose subject sits at the edges.

D

Cover and logo badge

My ideaRecommended

The banner stays a clean cover photo with nothing on top of it. A large logo badge overlaps its bottom edge, and the name and description sit beside it on the page, like a brand's profile page. Why I recommend it: no text ever sits on a photo, so it reads perfectly on every brand banner, including busy ones. The picture shows in full. It is the most compact option on a phone. A brand with no banner still looks complete, because the cover falls back to its brand colour.

E

Split: brand colour beside the picture

My idea

The left side is a solid block of the brand's colour, holding the logo, name and description. The banner fills the right side. No overlay at all, and every brand page gets a strong colour identity. On a phone the picture sits on top and the colour block below it.

F

Cinematic fade

My idea

A taller banner fades into the brand's dark colour at the bottom, with the logo, name and description in white on the fade. There is no box, so it feels like a magazine cover. It is the most dramatic option and needs banners that have room at the bottom.
