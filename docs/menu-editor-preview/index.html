Menu editor: three easier layouts

These are working previews built on your real menu: tap, type numbers, use the arrows. The sub-menu names and links inside Skincare and Brands are examples. Every layout drops the thin drop line. Each item gets a position number you can type, plus arrow buttons. Pick a letter. A is the layout you described.

A

Columns: every main menu item is a column

as you described

Every top-level item is a column side by side, like cards on a board; scroll sideways for more. Inside each column are its sub-menus and their links. Change a column's number or press ← → to move it. Change a link's number or press ↑ ↓ to move it.

Best for seeing everything at once. With 12 columns, you scroll sideways a lot.

B

Tabs, then that item's dropdown exactly as shoppers see it

alternative

The top row is your shop's menu bar. Tap one item, for example Skincare, and below it you work on just that item's dropdown. Its columns sit side by side the way the mega menu shows them on the site. Add a column, add links into a column, type numbers or press the arrows. You only ever look at the one column you are working on, which is what you asked for.

Why recommended: it matches the shop's own menu, so what you arrange is what shoppers get. Each screen only shows one item, so it stays light even with 17 brands. On a phone the columns stack one under another.

C

Two panes: the list on the left, the picked item on the right

A short list of the top-level items on the left, each with a number and arrows. The item you pick opens on the right with its sub-menus and links. It looks like a mail inbox.

Calm and simple. It doesn't show the dropdown's real shape.
